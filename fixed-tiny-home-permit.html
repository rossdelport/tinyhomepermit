Fixed tiny homes

Fixed tiny home permits in WA

Short answer

A tiny home fixed to the ground is a building, so it needs a building permit. Behind an existing house it follows the granny flat rules, which usually means no planning approval if it is 70 m² or less on a residential lot.

Last updated 29 October 2026

Fixed means it's a building

A tiny home on footings, piers or a slab is a building. That means it needs a building permit before it is placed or built, and it has to meet the National Construction Code like any home: structure, fire safety, ceiling heights, light and ventilation, and energy efficiency.

Behind an existing house: the granny flat route

A fixed tiny home on the same lot as a main house is an ancillary dwelling, or granny flat. Since April 2024 it doesn't need planning approval if it is 70 m² or less, on a residential lot, and meets the R-Codes' standard rules such as setbacks. Our sister site Granny Flat Permit covers this in detail.

As the only home on the lot

A fixed tiny home as the main home on a lot is assessed as a house. It needs a building permit, and planning approval where your zoning or the R-Codes require it. Rural lots often need extra approvals for water and wastewater.

Modular and transportable tiny homes

Factory-built tiny homes delivered by truck still need a building permit for your lot. The application covers the footings or tie-downs, the connections to services and the home itself, using the manufacturer's documents.

Questions

Common questions

Is a fixed tiny home cheaper to approve than a granny flat?

The approval process is the same, because it is the same type of building. The difference is usually in the build itself.

Can I put my tiny home on wheels onto footings later?

You can, but it then becomes a building and needs a building permit, and it must meet the building code. Some tiny homes built for the road won't meet it without changes.
