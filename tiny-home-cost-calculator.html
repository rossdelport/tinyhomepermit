What tiny homes cost to buy in Perth

The home itself is the biggest cost. Sellers advertise starting prices from about $26,500 (Everyday Tiny Homes) and $42,990 (Perth Tiny House "Bek"), to $84,990 (Treehab, 5 to 6 m) and $192,800 (Summit Tiny Homes). See tiny home prices in Perth for what each includes.

Costs people forget

  • Delivery. Loads wider than 2.5 m or higher than 4.3 m need a Main Roads permit. Homes built interstate cost more to move.
  • Site work. A level pad, and for a fixed home, engineered footings.
  • Services. Licensed trades to connect power, water and sewer, or a septic system where there's no sewer.
  • Approvals. A fixed tiny home needs a building permit and must meet the building code. One on wheels needs council approval to be lived in for more than 5 nights in 28 days.

How the fees are worked out

For a fixed tiny home the calculator uses the state fees for an uncertified (BA2) permit: 0.32% of the value of the work (at least $121), plus the Building Services Levy ($61.65 up to $45,000, then 0.137%), plus the Construction Training Fund levy (0.2% on work over $100,000). Questions

Common questions

How much does a tiny home cost in Perth?

Sellers advertise from about $26,500 to over $190,000 for the home itself. Delivery, site work, services and approvals add to that.

Do I need a building permit for a tiny home in WA?

A fixed tiny home on footings does. A genuine towable tiny home on wheels is a caravan and doesn't, but it needs council approval to be lived in long term.

How much is the building permit for a fixed tiny home?

For a BA2 it's 0.32% of the value of the work, plus the Building Services Levy and, over $100,000, the Construction Training Fund levy. The calculator works it out.

What costs do people forget with a tiny home?

Delivery, a level pad or footings, connecting power, water and sewer, and approvals.
