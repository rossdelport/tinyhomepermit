Tiny home vs caravan

Short answer

In WA, a tiny home on wheels fitted for living in is legally a caravan. Both follow the same rules: no building permit, 5 nights in 28 days without approval, up to 24 months with council approval, and wheels must stay on. Take the wheels off and it becomes a building.

Last updated 13 November 2026

In the eyes of the law, they're the same

The WA planning commission's tiny houses fact sheet says a tiny home on wheels that's fitted for living in is a caravan. A 2018 tribunal case (Henville v City of Armadale) found that something is either a vehicle or a building, not both. So a tiny home on wheels follows the caravan rules.

The rules that apply to both

Where they differ

The differences are practical, not legal. Tiny homes are usually built to look and feel like a small house. Weight matters, because caravan licence fees are worked out by weight ($7.44 per 100 kg in 2026/27). Check the tare weight before you buy.

If you take the wheels off and fix it to the ground, a tiny home stops being a caravan. It becomes a building, needs a building permit and must meet the building code.

What this means for you

  • For a short or temporary stay, a tiny home on wheels and a caravan have the same options.
  • For a permanent home, a fixed tiny home approved as a house or granny flat is the main way under current rules.

Not sure which yours is? Try the caravan or building checker, or read tiny home registration and living in a caravan on your own land.

Questions

Common questions

Is a tiny home on wheels a caravan in WA?

Yes. The WAPC's tiny houses fact sheet says a wheeled tiny home fitted for living in is treated as a caravan.

Does a tiny home on wheels need a building permit?

No, because it's a caravan, not a building. A fixed tiny home does need one.

Can I live permanently in a tiny home on wheels in WA?

Not under the current rules, outside a caravan park. Councils can approve up to 24 consecutive months at a time.

Do tiny homes need to be registered like caravans?

Yes. A tiny home on wheels is licensed with the Department of Transport, with fees based on weight.
