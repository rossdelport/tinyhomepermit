Shire of Mundaring tiny home rules

Short answer

On wheels, a tiny home is a caravan: you can stay 5 nights in any 28 days on private land, or up to 2 years with approval while you build a house on rural or large lots. Permanent living is only allowed in caravan parks. On footings, it can be approved as a house or granny flat with a building permit.

Last updated 29 October 2026

The Shire of Mundaring is one of the few WA councils with a page just for tiny homes. Its rules are a good guide to how most Perth hills and rural councils think. Here they are in plain English, with what they mean for you.

Tiny homes on wheels

If your tiny home is on a trailer or wheels, the Shire treats it as a caravan, however nicely it is built. That leads to three rules:

What you want to doThe Shire of Mundaring rule
Stay on private land for a short timeUp to 5 nights in any 28 days with the landowner's consent, no approval needed
Live in it while you build your houseUp to 2 years with an Application for Temporary Accommodation (see below)
Live in it permanentlyOnly in an approved caravan park
Rent it out (for example on Airbnb)Only in an approved caravan park

Living in it while you build

You can apply for Temporary Accommodation if you have a building permit for your house and the land is zoned Rural Residential, Rural Smallholdings or General Agriculture, or the lot is over 2,000 m². The tiny home must:

  • stay roadworthy and able to be moved off the site at any time, so you can leave in a bushfire;
  • be kept in a shed; and
  • be connected to permanent power, water and effluent disposal.

Tiny homes without wheels

A tiny home on permanent footings has a clear path to approval. The Shire treats it as a Single House, or as an Ancillary Dwelling (granny flat) if there is already a house on the lot. There is no minimum size for a Single House, so tiny is fine, but it has to work as a real home:

  • a proper kitchen (with a stove or oven and a sink), bathroom (basin, shower or bath, waterproofing, ventilation) and enclosed laundry (trough, waterproof floor);
  • minimum ceiling heights;
  • power, running water, and sewer or septic;
  • structural engineering certification;
  • energy efficiency compliance; and
  • a BAL (bushfire) report if the lot is bushfire-prone, which much of the Shire is.

The approvals, in order

  1. Environmental check. If the block is mapped as a Local Natural Area or has native vegetation or a watercourse, the Shire suggests an Environmental Asset Inspection. Book it for the same time as the BAL assessment.
  2. Health approval. On septic, Environmental Health approves the system for the extra kitchen and bathroom.
  3. Planning (development) approval. Likely needed if you are in a bushfire-prone area, on a heritage property, or if the design doesn't meet the standard R-Codes rules (setbacks, size and so on). For a $73 fee, Shire planners will do a preliminary check of your site plan, elevations and floor plan first.
  4. Building permit. Always needed for the tiny home itself.

Tiny homes as tourist stays

A tiny home on wheels can't be approved as tourism accommodation unless it is part of an approved caravan park. A tiny home without wheels can be considered on its merits as a tourism development.

What this means for you

  • Want to live in it long term? Put it on footings and approve it as a house or granny flat.
  • Building a house first? A tiny home on wheels can be your home on site for up to two years, if your lot and zoning qualify.
  • Bushfire-prone lot? Expect a BAL report and probably planning approval. Get the BAL done early, as it shapes the design.

Contact numbers from the Shire: Planning Services (08) 9290 6740, Building Services (08) 9290 6666.

Questions

Common questions

Can I live in a tiny home on wheels in the Shire of Mundaring?

Permanently, only in an approved caravan park. On private land you can stay up to 5 nights in any 28 days, or up to 2 years with Temporary Accommodation approval while your house is being built.

Is there a minimum house size in Mundaring?

The Shire says there is no minimum size for a Single House, so a tiny home on footings can be approved if it has a proper kitchen, bathroom and laundry and meets the building code.

Can I Airbnb a tiny home in the hills?

Not a tiny home on wheels, unless it is in an approved caravan park. A tiny home without wheels can be considered on its merits as a tourism development.

How much does the Shire's preliminary planning check cost?

The Shire lists a $73 fee for a preliminary assessment, with a site plan, elevations and floor plan.
