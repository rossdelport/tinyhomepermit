How to choose a tiny home builder in Perth

Short answer

First decide on wheels or fixed. For wheels, ask how it'll be licensed and moved. For fixed, check the builder is registered and handles the building permit. Get a written contract, pay no more than a 6.5% deposit, and be wary of "permit-free" claims.

Last updated 20 November 2026

Step 1: Decide on wheels or fixed

This changes which rules the builder has to follow. In WA, a tiny home on wheels is treated as a caravan. A tiny home on the ground is a building and needs a building permit and must meet the building code.

Step 2: Questions for a tiny home on wheels

  • Can it be licensed? Ask how the builder gets the trailer licensed with the Department of Transport. A trailer with a damaged or missing VIN can't be licensed.
  • Is it road legal size? Over 2.5 m wide or 4.3 m high needs a Main Roads permit to move.
  • Who delivers it? Ask who moves it and what's included.
  • Where can you live in it? Most councils limit living in a caravan on private land, so check with yours first.

Step 3: Questions for a fixed tiny home

  • Are they registered? Work on a home worth over $20,000 that needs a permit must be done by a registered builder or an approved owner-builder. Be careful of "permit-free" or "DIY" claims; the state warns they can be misleading.
  • Who gets the permit? Ask who handles the Certificate of Design Compliance and building permit.
  • Is there home indemnity insurance? It's required for permit work over $20,000. You can check the policy on the insurer's register.

Step 4: Check the contract and payments

  • For home building work between $7,500 and $500,000, the contract must be in writing and signed.
  • The deposit can be no more than 6.5%, and progress payments should only be for work done.
  • For modular homes, the state suggests a small deposit, payments tied to stages, asking for proof of progress, and visiting the site.

Step 5: Compare prices fairly

Advertised Perth prices in 2026 range from about $26,500 to over $190,000. Check what each price includes: delivery, connection to power and water, and permits are often extra.

Questions

Common questions

Does a tiny home builder in WA need to be registered?

For a fixed tiny home worth over $20,000 that needs a building permit, yes, unless you're an approved owner-builder. A tiny home on wheels is a caravan, so builder registration doesn't apply in the same way.

How much deposit should I pay a tiny home builder?

For a home building contract, the deposit can be no more than 6.5%. Progress payments should only be for work done.

What should I ask a tiny home on wheels builder?

Ask how it will be licensed, whether it's under 2.5 m wide and 4.3 m high, who delivers it, and what's included in the price.

Are permit-free tiny homes real?

There are no special exemptions for prefab homes in WA. A fixed tiny home needs a building permit, and the state warns that permit-free claims can be misleading.
