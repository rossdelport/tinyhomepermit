Question answer

Can you have more than one tiny home on your land?

Short answer

Not without approval. WA's regulations allow only one caravan (including a tiny home on wheels) to be used for camping on a lot at a time. Your council can approve more for up to 3 months. Longer than that needs the Minister's written approval. Breaking the rule can mean a fine of up to $2,000.

Last updated 9 November 2026

The rule: one caravan per lot

A tiny home on wheels fitted out to live in is treated as a caravan in WA. Regulation 12 of the Caravan Parks and Camping Grounds Regulations 1997 says the owner or occupier of a lot must make sure that no more than one caravan is being used to camp on the lot at any one time, unless they have written approval. The fine for breaking this is up to $2,000.

How longWho approves
Up to 3 monthsYour local council
More than 3 monthsThe Minister

Before giving approval, the council or Minister must be satisfied the land is suitable for camping, especially for safety, health and access to services.

Short stays vs long stays

  • Up to 5 nights in any 28 days: you can camp on land you own or have the right to use without approval (but still only one caravan at a time unless approved).
  • Longer, one tiny home: apply to your council, which can approve up to 24 consecutive months. If the council refuses, or sets conditions you don't accept, you can apply to the Minister within 35 days.
  • Longer, two or more tiny homes: needs the Minister's written approval if it's more than 3 months.

It must stay mobile

Regulation 15 says a caravan must keep its wheels and be able to be moved, under its own power or by towing, within 24 hours of its services being disconnected. Taking the wheels off, or making it impossible to move, can carry a fine of up to $5,000. A tiny home that can't move is a building, with very different rules.

What about fixed tiny homes?

A tiny home without wheels is a building, not a caravan, so the one-caravan rule doesn't apply. Instead, councils assess it under planning rules, usually as a single house or a granny flat (ancillary dwelling). The Shires of Mundaring and Serpentine-Jarrahdale both treat fixed tiny homes this way. A second fixed tiny home on a lot with a house would usually need to meet the granny flat rules, including the 70 m² limit, to skip planning approval. Councils vary, so ask yours.

Questions

Common questions

Can I have more than one tiny home on my land in WA?

Not on wheels without approval. The regulations allow only one caravan to be used for camping on a lot at a time. Your council can approve more for up to 3 months; longer needs the Minister's approval.

Who approves two tiny homes on wheels on one lot?

Your local council for up to 3 months. For more than 3 months, the Minister must give written approval.

What is the fine for too many caravans on a lot in WA?

Up to $2,000 under regulation 12 of the Caravan Parks and Camping Grounds Regulations 1997.

Can I take the wheels off my tiny home?

Not if you want it treated as a caravan. Regulation 15 says it must keep its wheels and be movable within 24 hours of disconnecting services. Otherwise it's a building.

Can I have two fixed tiny homes on one block?

Fixed tiny homes are assessed as a house or a granny flat. A second one on a lot with a house usually has to meet the granny flat rules, including 70 m² or less, to skip planning approval. Councils vary.
