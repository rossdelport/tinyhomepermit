Question answer

Does a tiny home on wheels need to be registered in WA?

Short answer

To be towed on the road and treated as a caravan, yes: license it with the Department of Transport and Major Infrastructure. You need a roadworthiness inspection, form VL17 and ID, lodged in person. Fees are $7.44 per 100 kg. Some councils, such as Swan, only approve licensed vehicles for living in.

Last updated 11 November 2026

Why registration matters

In WA, a tiny home on wheels is treated as a caravan only if it's a genuine vehicle, built to be moved, not just able to be moved. Licensing (registering) it with the Department of Transport and Major Infrastructure helps show it's a vehicle. Some councils ask for it: the City of Swan's temporary accommodation form accepts licensed vehicles only, with conditional approval if the registration is still pending.

A home on wheels that's too big to be towed on the road may instead be a park home. Park homes don't need a vehicle licence, but they can only be lived in inside a licensed caravan park.

How to license a tiny home as a caravan

  1. Get it inspected. Take it to an Authorised Inspection Station for a roadworthiness inspection. The department can't license a vehicle whose VIN has been removed or defaced.
  2. Fill in form VL17, Application to license a vehicle.
  3. Bring ID: one primary and one secondary document. First-timers without a WA driver's licence also fill in form VL186.
  4. Lodge in person at a service centre or regional agent, with the inspection certificate and proof of ownership (such as a receipt or contract of sale showing the VIN).

If the weight can't be confirmed from the maker's specifications or records, you'll also need a weighbridge certificate.

Example: a 4,000 kg tiny home at $7.44 per 100 kg is about $298 a year in licence fees, before the flat fee, recording fee and insurance.

Registration doesn't replace council approval

Licensing makes it a road vehicle. It doesn't give you the right to live in it anywhere. You can still only stay 5 nights in any 28 days without approval, or up to 24 months at a time with council approval. The wheels must stay on, and it must be able to be moved within 24 hours of the services being disconnected.

Questions

Common questions

Does a tiny home on wheels need to be registered in WA?

To tow it on public roads and be treated as a caravan, it should be licensed with the Department of Transport and Major Infrastructure. Some councils, such as Swan, only approve licensed vehicles for temporary accommodation.

How much does it cost to register a tiny home on wheels in WA?

A trailer-type caravan is $7.44 per 100 kg for 2026/27, plus a flat fee, recording fee and motor injury insurance. A 4,000 kg home is about $298 in licence fees.

How do I register a tiny home on wheels?

Get a roadworthiness inspection at an Authorised Inspection Station, fill in form VL17, and lodge it in person with ID, the inspection certificate and proof of ownership.

Does registration mean I can live in my tiny home anywhere?

No. It's still a caravan under the camping rules: 5 nights in any 28 days without approval, or up to 24 months with council approval.
