On wheels

Tiny homes on wheels: the WA rules

Short answer

In WA a tiny home on wheels is treated as a caravan. Since 1 September 2024, your local government can approve one on private land for up to 24 months at a time, and the approval can be renewed. More than one on a lot needs the Minister's approval.

Last updated 29 October 2026

A tiny home on wheels is a caravan

Under WA law, a tiny home built on a trailer is treated as a caravan. Living in one on private land is covered by the Caravan Parks and Camping Grounds rules, not the building permit system. That is good news: it means a simpler application, and the tiny home itself doesn't have to meet the building code.

What changed in September 2024

From 1 September 2024 the WA Government changed the caravan and camping regulations so that local governments can approve a caravan or tiny home on wheels on private land for up to 24 months. The approval can be renewed for further periods. Before that, long stays were much harder to get approved.

How long can you stay?

StayWho approves
A few nightsThe landowner
Longer, up to 24 monthsThe landowner and the local government
Beyond 24 monthsA new or extended approval from the local government
More than one caravan or tiny home on a lotThe Minister for Local Government

Councils differ in exactly how they apply the short-stay limit, so check with yours, or ask us.

Conditions councils set

  • Access to drinking water, a toilet and a shower
  • Safe disposal of grey water and sewage
  • Licensed power and gas connections
  • Fire safety and bushfire measures
  • Where it sits on the lot, and keeping it roadworthy

Some councils have their own tiny home policy, and some allow only one tiny home on wheels per property.

Decks, verandahs and carports

Permanent structures built next to a tiny home, like a deck, a verandah or a carport, are buildings. They may need a building permit, even though the tiny home doesn't.

Questions

Common questions

Does my tiny home on wheels need to be registered?

If it is towed on public roads, the trailer needs to be registered and roadworthy. Councils may also ask that it stays mobile, rather than being fixed to the ground.

What if my tiny home is taken off its wheels?

Once it is fixed to the ground on footings, it is usually treated as a building and needs a building permit. See fixed tiny home permits.

Can I live in a tiny home on wheels on vacant land?

Sometimes. Some councils allow it, often while you build a house, but conditions about water, toilets and waste are stricter when there is no existing home on the lot.
